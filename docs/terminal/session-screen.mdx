---
title: "The session screen"
description: "What opc draws, and how it fits in your terminal."
---

`opc` draws into your terminal's normal screen, not a full-screen overlay. Output scrolls like any other command's, and your terminal's scrollback, search and copy work as usual. When you leave, everything stays on screen above your prompt.

```
▸ cs 7f3a · flaky test in segment.test.ts · opus 5.5 · high · acceptEdits
you   fix the flaky test in segment.test.ts and open a PR
      I'll start by running the test a few times to see how it fails.
  ✓ Bash  npm test -- segment.test.ts --repeat 20         4.1 s
  ✓ Read  src/segment.ts                                   312 lines
      The test depends on timer order. `flush()` runs before the
      debounce settles when the machine is busy…
  ⠋ Edit  src/segment.test.ts
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
> │
● running · 2 queued                                    ctrl+o details
```

From the top:

- **The header** names the session, its model, effort and permission mode. It is printed once, when you attach, and again when a setting changes.
- **The transcript** is the conversation. Your messages are marked `you`, messages from other people carry their name and where they wrote from (`Mo · slack`). The agent's text is indented beneath.
- **Tool cards** are one line each: a status mark, the tool, a summary of its input and, when it ends, its duration or size. `⠋` is running, `✓` succeeded, `✗` failed.
- **The prompt line** is where you type. It is live from the moment `opc` starts, even before the connection is up.
- **The status line** under it says what the session is doing. See [Status line](/terminal/status-line).

## Text as it arrives

The agent's text appears as the model produces it, drawn on the next screen refresh, at most 16 ms later. Markdown is rendered as it streams: code blocks get a gutter, inline code and emphasis are styled, links are clickable in terminals that support them. A block is re-rendered once when it completes, so a table or list that streamed in pieces settles into its final layout.

Reasoning is hidden by default. `Ctrl+T` shows it, dimmed, above the text it led to.

## Tool cards

A card shows the tool and the most useful part of its input: the command for `Bash`, the path for `Read` and `Edit`, the pattern for `Grep`. `Ctrl+O` expands the most recent card to show its full input and output, and again collapses it. `/details` expands every card in the turn.

Subagents appear as a card with their own nested cards, collapsed to one line when they finish.

## When you attach to a session with history

`opc a` prints the session's earlier turns before the live part, so you see what led here. Long histories are summarized: the last three turns are printed in full and earlier ones as one line each. Your terminal's scrollback holds all of it.

## Messages from elsewhere

If someone writes in the Slack thread, or another terminal is attached, their messages appear in the transcript with their name, as they are received. Asks show who answered them. `opc` never hides activity from other clients.

## Narrow terminals and plain output

Lines wrap to the terminal width, and cards truncate their summary rather than wrap. Below 40 columns, the status line drops its right side.

When standard output is not a terminal, `opc` does not draw this screen. It prints the transcript as plain text and exits when the turn ends, as `opc -p` does. See [Scripting](/cli/scripting).

Colors follow your terminal's palette. `NO_COLOR=1` turns them off. `opc` uses 16 colors only, so themes look right in every terminal.
